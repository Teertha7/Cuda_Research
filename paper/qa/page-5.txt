VII. DISCUSSION
The primary ﬁnding is a difference between where arith-
metic runs quickly and where an entire host-to-host image
operation runs quickly. For a small regular stencil, device
computation is only part of the pipeline. Keeping intermediate
data resident on the GPU could change this balance, but it
would answer a different question and is not measured here.
Adding shared-memory trafﬁc and synchronization can cost
more than the reuse it enables. The observed result should
inform a future optimization choice, not be hidden by empha-
sizing a favorable isolated wall-time sample. Similarly, col-
laborative CPU–GPU execution should be compared against
both endpoints under repeated controlled conditions. Merely
demonstrating correctness and asynchronous submission is
insufﬁcient to claim a practical gain.
The model is useful as a compact description of this dataset,
while the rate-estimate regret demonstrates the limits of ideal-
ized divisible-work assumptions. The extensive scheduling lit-
erature provides stronger general techniques; our study instead
makes the timing scope and the limits of a small empirical
model inspectable.
VIII. THREATS TO VALIDITY AND LIMITATIONS
This is one laptop and one session. The desktop GPU is
shared, CPU placement is uncontrolled, and background ac-
tivity, thermal state and power policy can affect measurements.
Fifteen within-process repetitions characterize local variability
without establishing cross-day reproducibility. Table II reports
the largest-resolution spread so that small differences are not
mistaken for robust improvements.
Only synthetic images are measured. Natural-image gener-
alization, different machines, energy, GPU-resident pipelines,
cold-start latency and production-library baselines remain
untested. CPU SIMD tuning is not explicitly optimized.
OpenMP Canny retains a serial queue and includes intermedi-
ate allocation costs; CUDA and hybrid Canny are not imple-
mented. Exact comparison validates the speciﬁed algorithms,
not perceptual edge quality or agreement with every library.
Tests cannot prove absence of all memory errors, and Compute
Sanitizer was not run.
The model uses a small, structured dataset and conﬁguration
medians. MAPE alone does not validate an allocation decision,
and no statistically signiﬁcant or universally optimal partition
is claimed. The review veriﬁes source existence and records
reading depth, but is not an exhaustive systematic review.
Author information and human validation remain required
before submission.
IX. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK
Under the speciﬁed host-to-host timing contract, OpenMP
with eight threads outperforms baseline CUDA Sobel at the
largest tested size, despite a short CUDA kernel interval.
Shared-memory tiling worsens kernel time in all tested conﬁg-
urations. Hybrid allocation depends on image size and execu-
tion context; the evidence does not support a general advantage
over CPU-only execution. A small model extrapolates to the
held-out largest size with 6.13% MAPE, within a deliberately
narrow scope.
Next steps are independent measurement sessions, con-
trolled power and afﬁnity, natural images with documented
licenses, proﬁler-conﬁrmed overlap, allocation-inclusive la-
tency, and GPU Canny with validated global connectivity.
These extensions are required before broader performance or
scheduling claims.
